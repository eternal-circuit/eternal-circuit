*Register: grief inside an ordinary task (Rachel)*

Rachel packed the mugs because the mugs were easy. Four of them, two chipped, one from the clinic's fundraiser with a cartoon tooth on it that Daniel thought was the funniest object in California. She wrapped each in a page of newspaper from the stack the property manager had left, and she did not read the newspaper, and then she read it. A sale on outdoor furniture. A church supper. Somebody's lost dog, a terrier, answering to Biscuit.

She sat down on the kitchen floor with the tooth mug in her lap.

She tried telling herself it was the house, and the thought didn't fit. The house had been kind to her more often than it had been anything else. It was the week she would have had. The trail, the garden, the cupboard that stuck and which she had been going to learn. A version of herself who could find the light switch in the dark without reaching.

The kitchen faucet dripped once. She waited for the house to tell her about it.

It didn't. It had been disconnected. She cried then, briefly and practically, the way she did everything, and then she wrapped the mug.
