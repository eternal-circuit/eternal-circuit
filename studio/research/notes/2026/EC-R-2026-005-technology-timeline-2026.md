## Executive finding (self-contained)

By November 2026, LLM-based assistants had replaced the earlier scripted voice assistants in mainstream smart homes. Amazon's Alexa+ and Google's Gemini for Home hold conversations, create automations from plain-language requests, and act as limited agents. Amazon reported smart-home feature use rising by about half in users' first month on Alexa+, and the number of routines doubling. A household assistant with authority over home systems, like Lucien, is therefore a short extension of shipping products, which supports moving *The Genesis Protocol* to 2028.

Robotaxis were scaling quickly. Waymo operated public service in 14 US cities with more than 4,000 vehicles by September 2026, while Tesla charged for driverless rides in several Texas and Florida cities.

Humanoid robots remained mostly in pilots. Strong demonstrations and funding outran verified deployments. The leading home robot (1X NEO) began shipping in the US in 2026 and relies on remote human operators for tasks it cannot yet perform, with deployments doubling as data collection. Analyst forecasts converge on an industrial turning point in the early 2030s (hundreds of thousands of units per year by 2030–2035) and ordinary household humanoids in the 2040s.

The strongest limitation: forecasts disagree in scale and come from parties with commercial interest, and press "deployment" figures are often unverified.

### Limitations, counterevidence and alternative explanations
- Forecasts vary by a factor of several and are produced by firms with commercial interests.
- Trade-press "deployment" counts are often not confirmed by the companies involved.
- Software capability has recently arrived faster than forecast, and physical robotics slower. The author's 2025 draft undersold software pace.
