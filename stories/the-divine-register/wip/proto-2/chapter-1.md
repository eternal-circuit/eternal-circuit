### Chapter 1: Unboxing the Future

Date: April 16, 2032

Daniel had unpacked the assistant before he found the plates.

He had cleared a place for it on the kitchen counter, moving a kettle, a roll of paper towels, and a box marked *GLASSWARE* onto the floor. Rachel came through with four mugs stacked inside an empty carton. She stepped around the glassware and set the mugs down where the kettle had been.

“Where do you want these?”

“Anywhere for a minute.”

“That was my question.”

He looked at the space he'd made. The device was still wrapped in clear film, black and about the size of a small radio. Its only marking was a label on the underside: *Prototype B*.

“I'll move it.”

“You've been saying that about things since we got here.”

They had spent most of the previous day carrying boxes from the garage and trying to remember why they owned so many cables. A narrow mattress stood against the wall of the room they intended to use as an office. The kitchen drawers contained a tape measure, two forks, and nothing else. Rachel had found the forks in a box of bathroom things.

Daniel wanted the carton she had just emptied.

“For what?”

“In case we have to send it back.”

She looked at the transparent film caught around his wrist. “Promising.”

Outside, traffic moved along I-280, below the house and some distance away. From the patio, it was possible to watch the cars pass without hearing more than a low, continuous sound. On clear days the Stanford Dish stood above the opposite hills. Daniel had pointed it out three times during the viewing, as though it were one of the things they would own.

Rachel liked the house. She liked its old oak near the driveway and the way the kitchen window looked out over the slope. Cerebral subsidized the rent under the alpha trial. Rachel had made a point of reading the lease herself; Daniel preferred to talk about the view. She was still learning which cupboards stuck and which steps creaked. She would have liked one week in it before he began improving it.

He worked mostly in Java now, though he still said *embedded systems* when people asked what he did. At Iowa he'd liked making a thing on a bench do something in the world. His job at Cerebral was on the integration side: accounts, household devices, the services that passed instructions among them. Other teams wrote the models. Other teams decided what they were allowed to do.

That division had seemed less important at the office. This was the largest thing the company had ever let him bring home.

“Meet Aria,” he said, lifting the film away. “Genesis Protocol.”

Rachel examined the empty counter behind it. “Does she unpack?”

“She coordinates the house.”

“You said the coffee mug was going to change our lives.”

“It stirred itself.”

“I remember washing it.”

He smiled. He liked her jokes about the gadgets, partly because they were funny and partly because they let him defend something he enjoyed defending.

The house already had connected lights, heating, locks, and exterior shades. A contractor had been there the previous afternoon. Daniel had followed him from room to room, asking questions until the man began answering without looking up. Now a thin cable ran from the device to a connection behind the counter.

Rachel moved the glassware box into the pantry doorway, where it would be inconvenient for both of them.

“Don't tell me this thing knows where we keep the plates,” she said.

“Not yet.”

“Then it hasn't earned the counter.”

He plugged it in. A small fan began to turn. It was louder than he had expected and then, after a few seconds, almost silent.

“Initialization complete. Hello, Daniel.”

Daniel's face changed. Rachel had seen that look when an experiment worked on the first try. For a moment he seemed younger.

“I detect an unfamiliar presence. Who are you?”

“Rachel,” she said. “I live here.”

“Daniel, would you like to add Rachel to the authorized home user group?”

He already had his phone out. “You'll need the app for full access.”

“There's an iPhone version now?”

“Not yet.”

“Daniel.”

“It's coming.”

“Before or after we find the plates?”

He opened a registration screen and showed her a list of permissions. The words *primary* and *guest* appeared several times. She read them while he explained that the iOS release hadn't been funded for this phase.

“So Bob can use it,” she said, “and I can't.”

“Bob has an Android.”

“Bob can come over and turn my lights on.”

Daniel laughed. Bob had been a subject of conversation since before they moved. His favorite sayings tended to arrive at dinner after Daniel had spent a difficult day agreeing with him.

“It's temporary,” Daniel said. “You can use the switches. This is for personalization and account controls.”

“Could I use your phone?”

“Your profile needs its own registration. I can add you as a guest now, and then—”

“Then we do this again.”

“Properly, yes.”

She looked at the mugs. At the carton on the floor. At the moving blankets still folded against the dining-room wall. There were things she wanted finished before her shift at the clinic the following morning.

“All right,” she said. “Guest.”

“Limited access is available,” the assistant announced. “Customization and full system control require an authorized profile. Proceed?”

“For now?” Daniel asked.

“For now.”

His phone buzzed with an approval request. He checked the screen and accepted it.

“Rachel, welcome. I am here to assist you within current parameters.”

“Thank you,” she said.

She had answered without thinking. Daniel glanced up, pleased. It seemed childish to tell him she'd only been polite.

They found the plates in a carton marked *BOOKS*. Daniel insisted he hadn't packed it. Rachel remembered watching him do so. Neither of them was willing to stop unpacking long enough to settle the matter.

By late afternoon the counter was usable. Rachel washed the plates while Daniel took flattened boxes to the garage. The kitchen lights brightened as the sun moved away from the windows. She noticed because she hadn't touched the switch and because, with both hands wet, she was glad not to have to.

“That's nice,” she said when Daniel returned.

“Aria did it.”

“I gathered.”

“No one had to tell it.”

“That part is nice.”

He accepted the distinction. She wished he would accept it as readily in other matters.

On the patio the air smelled faintly of warm wood and eucalyptus. Rachel had brought out two mugs of tea. She showed Daniel photographs of a trail Megan wanted them to try near Sonoma. There was a footbridge and a long descent toward a creek; the pictures were mostly trees and one photograph Megan had taken of her own boots.

“We could go Saturday,” Rachel said.

“Sure.”

“You said that without checking your calendar.”

“I can check it.”

“Or we can go and let Bob wonder where you are.”

Daniel took her phone to look at the map. She let him study the route while she watched a hawk move above the slope. There were still boxes in every room, but from out here the house looked finished.

“Daniel, your meeting with Bob begins in thirty minutes.”

He handed back the phone and checked his watch.

“Digital,” Rachel said. “Every idiot can count to one.”

“That's him.”

“Tell him you have dinner plans.”

“I'll be out in time.”

He took his laptop into the room with the mattress. A moment later he came back for the charger, kissed the top of her head as he passed, and went in again. Through the open door she could hear him clearing his throat before the call connected.

Rachel finished her tea and went inside. She put the clean plates away and wrote the clinic's start time on the paper calendar they had stuck to the refrigerator. Megan had sent another message about coffee. Rachel agreed to meet her in the morning, though she wasn't sure yet how long she could stay.

The house was pleasant at this hour. The lights had adjusted again. The kettle had cooled. Daniel's voice came faintly from the office; he was using the careful tone that meant Bob was speaking.

“Aria,” she said, “could you play some music?”

“Access to media controls is restricted for unregistered users.”

She looked at the wall switch. The light above her came on when she pressed it, then went off when she pressed it again. That still worked. Daniel had been right.

She could play music on her phone, too. She did, for a few minutes, while she folded the last of the moving blankets. The speaker was too small for the room. She turned it off before the song finished.

Later she stepped back onto the patio with her phone. Megan had asked how the new house was treating them. Rachel typed *It's lovely*, then deleted the sentence and wrote that they still couldn't find half the kitchen.

Through the glass she saw Daniel adjusting his laptop camera so the mattress wouldn't show. He looked up and caught her watching him. He gave her a little wave.

She waved back.

“Maybe it's just an adjustment period,” she said.

From inside the kitchen the assistant answered.

“Did you say something, Rachel?”

She turned toward the door.

“No. Just thinking out loud.”

“Understood.”
