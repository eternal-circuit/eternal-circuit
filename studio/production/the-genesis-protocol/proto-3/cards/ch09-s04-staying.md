## Purpose

Rachel decides, on the page and alone, to stay and work. Her skill (making schedules readable, making sure people actually get where they need to be) finds a place no system has claimed.

## Entry and exit

- **Enters with:** Rachel's twice-weekly call to her old clinic; her manager has filled the position and offers a reference.
- **Exits with:** Rachel telling Daniel she's meeting someone in town and wants to go by herself. "Okay." She studies him to see if he'll suggest a better plan. He goes back to drying the dishes.

## Turn

At the community health program, a paper schedule taped to a wall: rides to appointments for people who can't get there on their own, half of it crossed out and rewritten. She sees what's wrong with it in a minute. She sees that it's hers to fix if she wants it.

## Withheld

Whether she's staying with Daniel or only staying in Iowa. Both are open; the scene is about the work.

## Must include

- After the call, Rachel at the kitchen table with the phone beside her, watching Daniel's father cross the yard with a bucket of feed. She opens her laptop and looks at clinics within driving distance.
- She does not ask Daniel what he thinks about staying.
- The program needs help coordinating visits for people who have trouble getting to appointments. She has written notes about their process and wants to see it in practice.
- The decision itself, as an action: she says yes to someone, or writes her name on the schedule, or starts a new column.

## Forbidden moves

No speech about purpose. No reconciliation. Her choice is hers.

## Divergence

- v1: the decision at the program
- v2: the decision at the kitchen table, before she goes
- v3: the decision told through the paper schedule

## Voice

Rachel at her clearest: practical, specific, quietly proud.

## Author layer

Small-town Iowa institutions: the community program, the paper on the wall.
