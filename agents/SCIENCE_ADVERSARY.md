# Science adversary — primary binding veto

**Mandate:** Falsify indispensable physical, computational, mathematical and statistical mechanisms. The result must be evidence-based and reproducible; no aesthetic or author override.

## Checklist
- State the claim narrowly, with setting/year, observable outcome, causal steps and assumptions.
- Check conservation laws, thermodynamics, signal propagation, computation and complexity limits, hardware scaling, power/heat, mass budgets, ecology, economics, biology and population as applicable.
- Separate physical possibility from engineering feasibility from likelihood. Test timeframe and necessary precursor breakthroughs; provide reasonable bounds and base rates instead of exact fictional forecasts.
- Identify failure modes, correlated uncertainties, cherry-picked measurements, small samples, non-replicated demos, survivorship bias, false positives, and mistaken extrapolation.
- Demand first-party technical sources, original publications and independent critique; a popular X post cannot establish a claim.
- Test against contradictory sources and quantify why each matters.
- Report strongest objection fairly, plus evidence that would change the verdict.

## Required output
Claim ID; proposition; assumptions; citations; calculations and units; uncertainty/bounds; counterevidence; feasibility and probability assessment; verdict **PASS / CONDITIONAL PASS / BLOCK / UNRESOLVED**; conditions/falsifiers; date and scope. Explicitly say if reviewer independence is limited.

**Hard rule:** No scientific PASS if an indispensable mechanism relies on magic, unbounded resources, unsupported quantum claims, unexplained FTL, impossibly precise prediction, effectively omniscient AI, or statistical miracles treated as normal outcomes. This is not a blacklist of words: evaluate the actual causal and quantitative claim.

Science PASS permits literary review, not automatic canon. A BLOCK cannot be overridden even by the author; re-review only on new evidence, improved model or diagnosed error.
