# Literary workflow

Use this workflow to develop outlines, scenes, chapters and revisions in *The Eternal Circuit*. The author can use it with one drafting collaborator or ask for focused independent readings.

1. **Set a brief.** Agree on the passage, the requested level of edit, point of view, what the character wants, what changes, and what the reader should know by the end. Identify applicable setting constraints and continuity anchors. Keep research detail outside the brief unless the scene needs it.
2. **Draft.** Start with action, perception, choice and consequence. Work in a proposal or visible diff and preserve the source version. A beat sheet, scene draft or line edit can each be the requested deliverable.
3. **Read and revise.** For structural work, first examine scene purpose, causality, character agency and emotional sequence. Then attend to viewpoint, pacing, dialogue, language and atmosphere where the brief calls for it. For a line edit, stay at the requested level. Use a distinct reader for a consequential craft question when helpful. Give the author the resulting passage and any remaining editorial choice.
4. **Verify against canon.** Check continuity and verify the passage’s consequential scientific, technical, timeline and economic claims. Use the author-approved setting record and its pinned scientific dossier or technical brief. Confirm the claim fits the reviewed era, domain, scale, assumptions and outcome. For a substantive draft or revision, keep a short working list of passage, claim, source revision and disposition. Ordinary changes to emphasis, point of view, dialogue and pacing reuse the existing check when the underlying claims stay the same.
   - **Verified:** Carry the claim forward within the recorded bounds.
   - **Contradicted:** Correct it against canon when the intended effect survives. If the desired passage depends on the contested claim, start a scoped universe task.
   - **Unverified:** Start a scoped universe task if the record is missing, provisional, conflicting, stale or outside the claimed scope. Send the exact passage and claim, relevant conditions, sources checked and what the scene needs to happen. Mark the dependency in the draft and continue work elsewhere or use a cleared alternative.
   - When the universe task returns, recheck the passage against its verdict and bounds. Where the result changes canon, obtain author approval before treating it as an established fact.
5. **Author decision.** The author chooses what to adopt. A canon PR records the approved diff and resolution of any outstanding scientific dependencies.

A scientific brief can establish what a device can do, who controls it, and what happens when it fails. A literary brief asks who discovers that failure, how much they understand, and what it costs them to act.
