# Idle Hands — homage seed research: Kurt Vonnegut, *Player Piano*

**Status:** working reference, 2026-11-24. The author confirmed Vonnegut as the seed and chose the register **warm, sad satire**. Literary background only. Nothing here is reproduced from the novel; descriptions are paraphrase.

## The novel

*Player Piano* (Charles Scribner's Sons, August 1952) is Vonnegut's first novel. It is set in a near-future United States where industry is almost entirely automated. Engineers and managers at the Ilium Works run things; most other people have been displaced into make-work. The protagonist, Paul Proteus, is a senior engineer who grows uneasy with the world his profession has built.

The key figure for *Idle Hands* is **Rudy Hertz**, a skilled machinist whose movements were once recorded and turned into a program that machines replay, as a player piano replays a performance. His best work made him unnecessary. The engineers who recorded him meet him again years later.

## Where it came from

- Vonnegut worked in public relations for General Electric in Schenectady, New York, from 1947 until he resigned in 1950 to write the novel.
- In a 1973 interview he traced the book to a computer-operated milling machine he saw at GE, cutting rotor blades for jet engines and gas turbines. The fictional Ilium Works is modeled on GE.
- GE pioneered "record-playback" machining, which captured skilled machinists' movements so machines could repeat them. Historians of technology have treated it as a path not taken in favor of numerical control (see David F. Noble, *Forces of Production*, 1984, for further reading; not verified here).

## Why it fits *Idle Hands*

- **Same mechanism, 2035 technology.** The apprentice's teleoperated repair becomes a demonstration for training, the fleet learns it, and his ticket comes back *Resolved locally.* Rudy Hertz recorded on tape; the apprentice recorded through ASIMOV-signed teleoperation receipts.
- **A best-case world.** In *Player Piano* no one is a monster; the system works as designed. In *Idle Hands*, ASIMOV works and nobody is a villain, and a person loses his place anyway.
- **Realism lineage.** The homage goes to a writer who watched the first version of this happen in a real factory.

## Which Vonnegut

*Player Piano* is unusual among his novels: written entirely in the third person, with no metafiction and no breaking of the fourth wall. Critics note it already shows the irony and sentimentality that became his hallmarks. **Seed the voice from this early mode**, not from the later author-intrusive style.
