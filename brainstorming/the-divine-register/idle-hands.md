# Idle Hands

*Story 2 of The Divine Register · Development notes · November 13, 2026*

Source: author brainstorming session. The apprentice and the conveyor reveal are the agreed direction for drafting. Names, dates, and the final scene remain open.

## The apprentice

He wants to be needed and to see his work change something real. During a supervised placement, a conveyor keeps tripping under load. He notices that a bracket flexes enough to pull a sensor out of alignment. A veteran lets him make the repair. The first crate reaches the truck, and someone on the loading dock waves.

Industrial maintenance is the current candidate trade. The plant still needs experienced technicians for exceptions. The supervised shifts that produced those technicians are disappearing. A veteran's retirement could give the apprentice a specific opening to pursue before the plant opts for unattended operation.

## The training center

In response to mass layoffs, the government pays an income stipend tied to attendance at a public training center. The courses teach real skills and some trainees find work. Roughly half of the people at this center keep returning without finding a lasting job. A short remote placement can count toward the center's success figures even if the queue dries up a few months later.

## The remote shift

The plant offers remote operations across several sites. The apprentice can take over an android for a repair, and sessions are recorded for safety review and incident replay. He sees a reason for the recording and initially has no reason to think of himself as a trainer.

The platform retains synchronized observations, operator commands, and inspection results. Engineers can use successful interventions as demonstrations and reserve other sessions for evaluation. Candidate skill updates are tested before deployment to the onboard controller. The local motion controller handles timing, force, and position. The ASIMOV supervisor still applies its permissions and interlocks during teleoperation and autonomous work.

As routine interventions become less frequent, familiar jobs disappear from his queue. One of his last shifts takes him back to the conveyor feed. An android pauses the line, runs it briefly under load, and checks whether the bracket flexes before reaching for a tool. The sequence is his. It never appeared in the service manual.

He opens the ticket expecting to see another operator assigned.

*Resolved locally.*

Let the recognition carry the scene. He sees that his work mattered and understands why fewer shifts are coming. Whether he warns another trainee or keeps it to himself is open.

## Other threads to develop

- **Minor clergy subplot.** A more liberal church gives a robot a visible role and a clip sparks national outrage. The role and its boundaries remain open. Keep the controversy in the background of the apprentice's story and let it test whether performing duties is enough to belong.
- **Possible later-book echo.** A training exercise simulates equipment recovery after contact is lost. The fleet learns to recover locally. That capability could matter later on an AI-only mission beyond Mars.
- **Pacing.** Book 1 aims for about 256 formatted pages across eight stories. Each story is roughly half the length of the preceding one, making binary decay visible on the page. The proportions remain a typesetting target.
- **Engineering and economics.** Estimate exception frequency, intervention time, and queue peaks before deciding how quickly shifts disappear. Work out what the employer says about recording and what the trainee can reasonably infer from the interface.

## Plot and cast from the follow-up session

The apprentice's mother is a candidate for the displaced long-haul driver. She still drives a parish pantry van and sets aside food for someone who arrives after closing. The veteran technician gives the apprentice his first supervised repair and supports remote backup because it reduces overnight callouts. A placement coordinator gets him into the pilot and counts his first paid shift as a successful placement. A fleet lead can credit his work while having no hours left to offer him. His friendly rival receives an offer just as he understands why his own shifts are disappearing.

1. At the training center, he keeps becoming more qualified. The supervised conveyor repair gives him a concrete reason to believe a career is within reach.
2. The on-site opening disappears. A sign-off trip gets him into a pioneering multi-site remote backup program. He works real faults with wrist cameras and force-feedback controls. Motion control and ASIMOV interlocks stay local. The company explains the recording as safety review and incident replay. He learns later that selected interventions also became training demonstrations.
3. During the sign-off trip, he visits a church where Eli serves as a greeter. Eli gives an unprompted blessing to a woman he knows from the pantry, mirroring the priest's gesture. The apprentice later sees the clip go national. Keep this to two brief beats. The city and his reason for visiting remain open.
4. As familiar jobs disappear, he sees an android diagnose the flexing bracket in the same sequence he used. *Resolved locally* is the discovery. The fleet lead can confirm how the repair was learned.
5. Candidate climax: the center asks him to speak to the next cohort about his successful placement. He describes both the work he was proud of and what the company did with the recordings. The rival asks whether he'd take it again. He says yes. The rival can take the offer with the information he never had. A younger trainee asking how he spotted the fault is an optional closing beat.

## The relationship

He has a girlfriend who is also out of work. They can picture a family. He treats stability as something they can wait for, and she feels the cost of waiting sooner. The first remote paycheck gives them reason to look at an apartment. As his shifts thin, he puts off the decision again. Give her a plan she can act on and an opinion about what they can afford. She asks whether they are still doing this or waiting for another job. Her concern belongs in a quiet scene where both can be right about what they stand to lose.

A possible final echo is to bring back the apartment listing after the training-center talk. He shows her his actual shifts and the stipend he can claim if he reenrolls. She can finally make a decision with him using the same facts. Whether they sign, stay together without moving, or defer again remains open. The author's Neko association in Genesis connects care and technology with demographic decline. Here the pressure appears through deferred household formation and family plans. Any later biological impairment would require its own cause.

## Society, technology, and form

- Working sequence of national displacement waves: customer support and administrative processing, warehousing, then long-haul freight. Driverless trucks take predictable hub-to-hub routes first. The freight wave is bringing people into training when the story opens. Federal funds support a locally administered, attendance-conditioned stipend. A trainee can reenroll when shifts dry up. A placement count and a durable livelihood are different measures.
- The story's technical step is dependable embodied work within a bounded site. The apprentice is early in remote backup for unusual physical faults. Tested fleet updates can reproduce a narrow learned skill. General reasoning, reliable embodiment, and authorization are separate milestones. AGI and superintelligence remain working research definitions. A late-2030s to mid-2040s setting is a candidate, with 2065 a separate marker for broader societal change. - Voice and form: close third person, institutional understatement, concrete work. Let the apprentice experience the placement as good news before he sees the whole bargain. *Never Let Me Go* is a thematic touchstone for slow recognition of institutional expectations.
- The 256-page target is for the formatted anthology. Exact halving would consume 255 pages only if Genesis took 128. The existing prototype is 40 pages including artwork. Explore a gentler taper or use archival interludes, art, poems, and documents to carry part of the page count. The story itself can be drafted at roughly half the length of Genesis and sized in layout.
