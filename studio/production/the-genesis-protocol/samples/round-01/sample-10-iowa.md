*Register: Iowa, a machine allowed to fail (Daniel)*

The tank sensor on the Hendersons' place had been lying about the water level since before Daniel was born. Empty, it said, through two droughts and one flood. Mr. Henderson drove out every morning anyway and looked.

"Why not replace it?" Daniel asked.

Henderson thought about it. He was a man who thought about things with his hat.

"I know what it means when it says empty," he said. "It means nothing. A new one, I'd have to learn what it means."

They stood at the tank in the wind. The corn was ankle-high and very green, and the sky was doing what it does in Iowa in June, being larger than anywhere else without apologizing for it. Daniel put his hand on the side of the tank and felt the cold of the water through the steel, all the way up past where the sensor swore it ended.

He thought about telling Henderson about cells and gates and signed receipts. He thought about how long that conversation would be.

"Full," Daniel said.

"Yep," said Henderson, who had known.
