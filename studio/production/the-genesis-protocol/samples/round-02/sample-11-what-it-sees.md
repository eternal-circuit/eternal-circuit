*Register: what does it see? (Rachel, with Tom; precise technical grounding)*

"What does it actually see?" Rachel asked. "Not what it does. What it sees."

Tom put down his sandwich. He seemed to like the question, which she hadn't expected.

"In this house? Occupancy from the motion sensors. Door states. Power draw by circuit. Audio, but not audio the way you'd think. Features. Energy in frequency bands, speech or not speech, who's talking if it's been taught the voices. Timestamps on all of it."

"Does it see me?"

"It sees a pattern it labels with your profile." He thought. "It knows you were in the kitchen at 6:58. It knows the kettle drew fifteen hundred watts for four minutes. It knows somebody said a sentence."

"Not which sentence."

"Only if the conversation layer was listening. Then a summary of the sentence." He picked the sandwich up again and didn't eat it. "So it sees that you were here, very precisely. It's never once seen why."

Rachel looked at the console under the cabinets, small and black and quiet.

"That's worse," she said.

"It's the same," Tom said. "We just usually say it the other way."
